# AI and the future of human decision-making

As AI transforms decision-making, how can organizations make quality decisions, anchored in human agency and trust?

By David Mallon, Julie Duda, Stefano Besana, Maya Bodan

9-min read•4 March 2026

![AI and the future of human decision-making banner image](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/insights/articles/2026/us188560_ghct2026_human-decision-making/content-images/US188560_Mobile-375x344.png)

## Introduction

AI has the potential to transform human decision-making. However, organizations should first treat this process as a strategic discipline and then design human–machine decision-making relationships accordingly. 

These stories illustrate important questions: What could have been done differently? How do organizations improve input and oversight when AI is involved in decisions? What is the best mix of AI and human in each decision, leveraging enough machine autonomy to improve speed, consistency, and scale while also maintaining sufficient human agency?

### Running with scissors: AI and decision-making
Leaders today face a torrent of choices in conditions that are noisier, faster, and riskier than ever. ... Today’s cautionary tales can become tomorrow’s competitive advantage.

According to a 2023 Oracle study, 85% of business leaders regret or question decisions they had made in the past. Additionally, 72% of those leaders say the volume of data and their lack of trust in data has stopped them from making *any* decision at all.

Many are turning to AI as a solution. In our 2026 Global Human Capital Trends survey, 60% of executives now regularly use AI to support their decisions.

### Elements of a decision framework

| Element | Defining Question | Implications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk profile | - Potential consequences and reversibility | - Higher risk: Greater diligence and consultation |
| Frequency | - Often-made or one-off | - Frequent: Opportunities to experiment or automate<br>- One-off: Bespoke analysis and explicit leadership calls |
| Urgency vs. importance | - Time sensitivity vs. strategic value | - Route as: Do-now, schedule, delegate, or ignore. |
| Horizon | - Timing of consequences<br>- Cyber | - Align KPIs, funding, and risk to the time horizon: win now vs. build future<br>- Cyber |
| Certainty/risk/uncertainty | - Knowns vs. predictable unknowns vs true unknowns | - Knowns/quantifiable: model risk<br>- Uncertain: use adaptive playbooks. |
| Level of chaos (Cynefin) | - How obvious is connection between cause and effect | If environment is…<br>- **Clear:** Use best practice<br>- **Complicated:** Use expert analysis<br>- **Complex:** Probe-sense-respond<br>- **Chaotic:** Act to stabilize |
| Option-creating vs. commitment | - Expands or constrains future choices | - Expands: Treat like experiment<br>- Commits: Treat like capital investments. |
| Guardrail-critical vs. value-seeking | - Degree of sensitivity, regulation, or special requirements for decision makers (i.e., certification) | - Guardrails: Use rules & audits<br>- Value-seeking: Use creative risk-return calculus. |

### Elevating both humans and machines as decision makers

Organizations should design for the decision-making competence they need: building human decision-making skill and measuring AI performance with rigor befitting their mission-critical contributions. 

### Design for human agency

**Build accountability through human agency.** Clearly connect choices to choosers, giving decision-makers confidence grounded in comprehension (not mere compliance).

**Determine the level of AI agency based on desired outcomes.** Design how much autonomy AI agents have based on the risk profile of the decision.

### Decision-making that works for humans and machines

Organizations that elevate decision-making... Technology can accelerate analysis and clarify uncertainty, but it cannot replace human purpose, values, and judgment behind choices.
