  2. The Bag of Words pipeline overviewThe BOW pipeline consists of cleaning text, tokenizing, building a vocabulary, and generating vectors that can be fed into a machine learning algorithm.
  3. From sentences to word frequency vectors by exampleSentences can be converted first into lists of words and then into dictionaries that count how many times each word appears in a sentence.
  4. Building a combined document vocabularyA combined word-frequency vocabulary is built from all documents, and each sentence vector is initialized as zeros with a length equal to the total vocabulary size.
  5. Populating sentence vectors from the vocabularySentence vectors are filled by iterating through the vocabulary and recording the count of each word that appears in the sentence, producing a fixed-length numeric representation.
  6. Sparse vectors and their challengesLarge vocabularies produce sparse vectors with many zeros, which require more memory and computation and can challenge traditional modeling algorithms.
  7. Tokenization and stopword removal in PythonTokenization breaks sentences into tokens while removing punctuation and stopwords; a basic implementation uses a custom ignore list and regex, while a more robust one can use NLTK's predefined stopword sets.
  8. Applying tokenization to all sentences to build a word listThe tokenize function collects unique, sorted words from all sentences by extending a list with each sentence's extracted words and then deduplicating and sorting them.
  9. Generating Bag of Words vectors in codeThe generate_bow function builds the vocabulary, then for each sentence creates a zero vector and increments positions where words match the vocabulary, producing vectors usable in machine learning.
  10. What BOW captures and ignoresBOW ignores meaning, context, and word order, so document similarity is determined purely by similar word counts.
  11. Limitations of Bag of WordsBOW's main limitations are loss of semantic meaning and context, and very large vector sizes for big documents that require ignoring words by relevance.
  12. N-grams and using existing librariesBOW can be extended with n-grams like bi-grams for better representation, and is already available in libraries such as scikit-learn's CountVectorizer, though understanding the internals improves framework usage.
